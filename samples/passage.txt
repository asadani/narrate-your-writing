READ THIS ALOUD to record your voice reference.

Narration pace - as if teaching, not chatting. Read at your NORMAL speed:
the clone copies your rhythm, so a carefully-slow reading gives you a
carefully-slow narrator for every article you ever render.

Leave a second of silence before you start and after you finish.
Mistakes are fine. Do NOT re-record to make it perfect - three
increasingly stiff takes cost you more than one fluffed word.

Record on your PHONE, not a Bluetooth headset and not your laptop mic.
Both of those cap the microphone at 8 kHz and the clone will sound muffled.
Then run:  python check_reference.py your-recording.m4a --trim 6

A protocol is just an agreement about how two programs talk.

A client is any program that wants something; a server is any
program that has it and is listening.

Underneath, the messages are JSON-RPC two point zero. Send a
method, some parameters, and an ID; get back a result or an error.

For remote servers the specification adopts OAuth two point one,
and the host runs a real authorisation flow in a browser.

Locally it's simpler: the host launches the server as a subprocess,
and they exchange newline-delimited JSON over standard input and
output. No ports, no network, no TLS.

The model does not run the tool. It cannot.

It produces a block of text saying which tool it would like called,
and with what arguments, and then it stops, and it waits.

Your code executes the call. Your code sends the result back.

Zero, one, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine.

Is it going to rain tomorrow?

------------------------------------------------------------

Why these particular lines: they cover the prosody a narrator needs.
Long subordinate clauses, a semicolon's mid-sentence lift, a colon
setting up a list, two very short declaratives for the falling cadence,
digits in sequence, and one real question at the end. Miss the question
mark and every question you ever narrate will come out flat.
